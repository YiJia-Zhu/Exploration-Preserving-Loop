D IVERGENCE CONTROLS ENTROPY IN DISTILLATION
Nicolas Zucchet and Scott W. Linderman
Stanford University
{nzucchet,slinderman}@stanford.edu

arXiv:2610.03529v1 [cs.LG] 2 Oct 2026

A BSTRACT
Distillation has become a core primitive of large language model training, but its
properties are not yet well understood. We take an entropic perspective, studying
how the entropy of the student depends on the data and the divergence that define
the distillation objective. We prove that forward KL inflates the entropy of the
student above that of the teacher. Since cross-entropy training is a special case,
this yields an identity that we verify quantitatively in pretraining and supervised
finetuning. Other divergences come with no such guarantee: reverse KL deflates
entropy until the gap between student and teacher gets too large, and interpolating
between the two changes entropy smoothly early in training but abruptly at convergence. The lower entropy of on-policy distillation comes from token-level reverse
KL, not from on-policy sampling. The divergence therefore acts as an implicit
entropy regularizer, whose role is clearest in self-distillation: as conditioning on
privileged information deflates entropy, the divergence hyperparameters that work
best are those that compensate for it.

I NTRODUCTION

Distillation consists of training a student model to mimic the behavior of a teacher model and compress its knowledge (Hinton et al., 2015). It is used in increasingly diverse contexts for large
language models, from pretraining lightweight, more efficient models (Muralidharan et al., 2024;
Gemma Team, 2025), to solidifying reasoning abilities in posttraining (Ma et al., 2026; DeepSeekAI, 2026). In practice, distillation amounts to minimizing a divergence between two categorical
probability distributions. While divergence minimization has been extensively studied elsewhere
(Minka, 2005; Bishop, 2006; Amari, 2016), its properties are not yet well understood in the context
of large language models.
In this work, we study the properties of these algorithms from an entropic perspective, investigating
how design choices affect the entropy of the student. Entropy is fundamentally a measure of uncertainty, whether in thermodynamics (Boltzmann, 1877) or information theory (Shannon, 1948), and
it is particularly important for large language models as it relates to the diversity of the output distribution (Kirk et al., 2024; Farquhar et al., 2024) and controls exploration in reinforcement learning
(Cui et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2025). It is also easy to compute and log during
any training run. Here, we develop intuition for how distillation shapes entropy.
Our contributions are as follows:
– Decoupling sampling and divergence choice (Section 2). We review distillation, decoupling
the choice of the data generation process from that of the divergence, which enables us to study
their effects on the student’s entropy independently.
– From divergence to entropy (Section 3). In a toy model of language modeling, we show
that: forward KL inflates the student’s entropy above the teacher’s by the residual divergence;
reverse KL deflates it until mimicking the teacher gets too difficult and then suddenly inflates
it; and interpolating between the two varies entropy smoothly early in training but abruptly at
convergence. Top-k restrictions also shift entropy, depending on how the tail is handled.
– At scale, the divergence implicitly controls entropy (Section 4). After pretraining and supervised finetuning, the entropy of a language model matches the cross-entropy loss it was
trained to minimize; larger models therefore have lower entropy. When distilling from a larger
teacher, the divergence drives entropy more than on- vs. off-policy sampling; in self-distillation,
it counters the entropy drop that conditioning on privileged information induces.
