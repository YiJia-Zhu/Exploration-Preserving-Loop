Abstract
On-policy distillation (OPD) consolidates expert capabilities into a single student by matching teacher
and student final-layer distributions on student-generated rollouts. We investigate whether depthwise
prediction transitions, the changes in predictions between layers, can provide additional task-relevant
supervision. Our diagnostic study finds that stronger teacher–student agreement in intermediate transitions
is associated with higher answer correctness within questions, with correct responses exhibiting lower
mean transition KL across all measured intermediate layer pairs. These observations motivate On-Policy
Transition Distillation (OPTD), which augments final-layer distribution matching by aligning teacher and
student layer-to-layer log-probability increments on student-generated rollouts. Evaluations across three
backbones and eight benchmarks spanning mathematics, coding, and instruction following show that
OPTD achieves the best or tied-best overall performance in both dual- and three-domain distillation. In
the three-domain setting, OPTD recovers ≈ 90% of the overall teacher–student capability gap, compared
with ≈ 75% for OPD.

from expert teachers into a unified student, as exemplified by DeepSeek-V4 and GLM-5 [1, 2]. Specifically, it
applies dense supervision at every token position of a
student-generated rollout by comparing the teacher’s
and student’s distributions conditioned on the same prefix, and uses the resulting KL-derived token-level reward
to guide policy distillation [3]. Grounding supervision
